Verdict by catalog size

  • Under 50 SKUs: Shopify Magic or ChatGPT. Do not pay a catalog vendor.
  • 50–500 SKUs: run the cost-per-SKU math. Describely publishes $0.75 per product pay-as-you-go (first five free) as of 21 August 2026. That is $37.50 at 50 SKUs and $375 at 500 — compare that to a year of seats.
  • 500+ SKUs: you are buying publish-back, enrichment, and a review queue. Hypotenuse and Describely’s high-volume custom tier are the aisle. Jasper and Writesonic are not.

Do not buy if

  • You have 20 products and a weekend. Magic or ChatGPT will empty that list.
  • You expect the model to invent missing specs. That is how you ship hallucinated wattage.
  • You need Amazon copy and you will not run a banned-word pass. The tool will not save you.
  • You are buying Jasper Pro to “do the catalog.” Read the next section.

Start here by store size

  • Shopify, small catalog: Magic for a week. Then decide.
  • Growing catalog, no PIM: Describely PAYG or Hypotenuse Basic (custom quote, <100 products on their public ladder).
  • Team + PIM / many channels: Hypotenuse Enterprise or Describely high-volume — ask them about publish-back to your stack.

Shopify Magic vs a dedicated catalog tool

Shopify documents Magic as a free suite of AI features inside admin, on all plans, with availability varying by surface. It will draft a product description in context. It will not, as a catalog system, give you CSV-in / Shopify-out for 400 SKUs, a shared taxonomy, or a queue. That is the whole comparison.

Use Magic when: you are writing the PDP you are looking at, brand rules fit in a prompt, and SEO title/meta can wait.

Leave Magic when: new-product intake is a weekly batch, you sell on more than one channel, or two people need the same voice without sharing a ChatGPT tab. Then you are in Describely / Hypotenuse territory — or you hire a writer and a PIM, which is still a valid answer.

Sidekick overlaps as an admin assistant, not as a catalog writer. See the Shopify AI apps page for native vs paid.

Describely

Describely is a catalog writer that charges like a catalog writer. Official pricing on 21 August 2026: pay-as-you-go $0.75 per product up to 500 products, unlimited generations on those products, data enrichment at $0.55/credit, image processing at $0.05/credit, store connectors included. High volume (500+ SKUs) is custom and includes an “AI Visibility Audit” on their page. First five products free, no credit card for the trial.

Cost-per-SKU math (PAYG list price, no discounts assumed): 80 SKUs ≈ $60. 300 SKUs ≈ $225. 500 SKUs ≈ $375. A year of Jasper Pro at $59/mo is $708 for one seat and still no publish-back. That is why Describely belongs on this page and Jasper belongs on the marketing page.

We have not run 25 SKUs through it. We will not invent an error rate. Their own “best generator 2026” blog ranks themselves #1; treat that as vendor content, not a third-party test.

Jasper / Writesonic as marketing writers, not catalog systems

Both will produce a product description if you paste a spec. Neither is a catalog system. Jasper Pro is a seat. Writesonic Starter is an AI-visibility plan with 15 articles per month on the public ladder — that is a blog quota, not a 400-SKU onboarding quota.

Buy Jasper or Writesonic when the same person also owns campaigns, landing pages, and (for Writesonic) AI-search tracking. Do not buy them to empty a spreadsheet. Alternatives context: Jasper alternatives.

Free one-off tools (and why they fail at 200 SKUs)

The free box on a vendor homepage, a “description generator” Chrome toy, and ChatGPT without a project all fail the same way at 200 SKUs: no shared attributes, no status (“draft / needs spec / live”), no publish-back, no banned-word library, and no owner. You will re-prompt. You will paste into Shopify by hand. You will ship two voice systems. The tool was never the bottleneck. The queue was.

Quality checks

  • Hallucinated specs. If the input row is empty, the model will fill it. Require a “spec present / spec missing” flag. Missing stays missing.
  • Units and variants. 12 oz vs 12 fl oz. EU vs US sizing. One wrong unit is a return.
  • Banned Amazon words. Amazon’s prohibited claims list is not optional. “Best,” “#1,” “guaranteed,” and medical language will come out of any model that has read the open web. Keep a deny list and run it before upload.
  • Channel fields. A Shopify body_html is not a Walmart key feature bullet. If the tool cannot emit per-channel templates, you are copy-pasting again.

Who should stay on ChatGPT

Founders under 50 SKUs. Anyone whose “catalog” is a collection page plus twelve products. Anyone who will edit every line anyway. Anyone who has not written a style sheet of three good PDPs — a $200/mo tool will not invent taste you have not defined.

When ChatGPT starts to hurt: a second person, a weekly intake larger than you can paste, or a channel that rejects your field format. That is the upgrade trigger. Not a listicle title.
